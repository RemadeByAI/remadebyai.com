    About layerling

    layerling works the way Tinkercad does (a build plate, solid shapes and hole shapes, group, bundle, Alt-drag to copy) and then goes further: chamfers and fillets on any edge, hollowing to an even wall, splitting, sketches you can extrude or revolve, threads, gears, patterns, hole grids and holes for heat-set inserts. It knows the plate size of more than 230 printers, marks overhangs, runs a print check for thin walls and floating parts, and exports STL, 3MF, OBJ and STEP. It imports STL, OBJ, 3MF, STEP, SVG and DXF.

    There is nothing to sign up for: designs stay in your browser unless you save them as a project file. You can install it as an app that starts without internet, or host your own copy with Docker on a PC, NAS or home server. An optional MCP server lets an AI client such as Claude or Codex build in the open editor while you watch. It is not a full CAD package: there is no parametric timeline and no assemblies.

    What people say

    Press coverage is small: All3DP wrote up the launch in September 2026, mostly describing it, with a dry nod to its fast pace of updates. The real opinions come from its very busy GitHub Discussions (more than 1,100 threads), where many people say they have switched from Tinkercad and find it easier and more capable. The criticism there is the normal stuff of a young, fast-moving app: bug reports and designs lost because nothing is saved to the cloud. Most of the feedback comes from enthusiastic users asking for features.

    People like

    • Many Tinkercad users say they have switched and prefer it
    • Easy to get into, even for beginners
    • Tools Tinkercad lacks, such as hollowing and fillets
    • Very frequent updates

    Common complaints

    • Bugs typical of a young app, for example exports ignoring a renamed design
    • Designs live in the browser, so unsaved work can be lost; some users want accounts or cloud saving
    • Mouse navigation differs from Bambu Studio by default
