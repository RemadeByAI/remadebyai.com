    About DesktopWiki

    DesktopWiki gives a family or small team a private wiki the way classic team wikis work: spaces with a page tree, a visual editor, templates, labels, comments and @mentions, page history with compare and restore, and search that reaches inside PDFs and scanned files. It also has databases with table, board, card and timeline views, whiteboards, diagrams, slides, per-space blogs, approvals, rules, a family calendar, chores with stars and rewards, and export to Word, Markdown or PDF. You install it on one Windows PC, and everyone else opens it in a browser on a phone or another computer.

    It includes a password manager with private and shared vaults (AES-256 and Argon2id), built-in two-step codes, password health checks, emergency access, sharing by link, and import from 1Password, Bitwarden, LastPass, KeePass, Dashlane and browsers. A personal start page brings links, weather, news, countdowns, to-dos and a wall display mode for a tablet. Home network monitoring checks your devices and internet, tells router, line and DNS faults apart, and tracks screen time on kids' PCs, consoles and TVs.

    There is no server, database or cloud account to set up: the whole wiki lives in one file you can back up by copying it, and it does encrypted daily or weekly backups too. It can import Confluence spaces and whole-site backups, plus Word, spreadsheets, Markdown and web pages. A free desktopwiki.net address, Tailscale or port forwarding can open it up for use away from home. It comes in nine languages, and AI agents can connect with read-only access.

    It is free for one person. Family (up to 6 people) is $49 a year, Team (up to 18) $99 and Business (up to 60) $199, on sale after the beta ends on December 31, 2026, with every feature in every plan. Everyone who ran the beta gets their first year of Family free. The host PC needs 64-bit Windows 10 (version 1809 or later), Windows 11 or Windows Server 2019 or later.
