    About Lightkeeper

    You aim the beam with the mouse, a finger or the keyboard. Holding it on a ship lets it chart a safe route to the harbour as far as the light reaches, a Flash lights the whole sea for a moment at the cost of oil, and three wrecks lose the night. Each night adds something new: fog banks, lightning, currents, wind, smugglers, supply ships and a kraken that rises under neglected ships.

    Between nights, letters arrive from the people you saved and the harbour sells upgrades such as a faster mechanism, a Fresnel lens, a rescue boat and a fog siren. All seven nights take about 15 to 20 minutes, progress is saved in the browser, and it is best played in landscape with sound on. All the art is drawn in code in a woodcut style.

    How it was built with AI

    The maker says on the jam page: "One person, zero hand-written code. I designed the game and made every call; AI agents built the rest in 13 days." The game's credits read "Code, art and tools: written with Claude (Anthropic) in Claude Code", with music from Suno and sound effects from ElevenLabs (https://itch.io/jam/ai-jam-5/rate/5265063).

    What people say

    The feedback comes from players on itch.io, mostly fellow entrants in the AI Browser Game Jam 5, where it tied for first place overall from 14 ratings. Players praise the melancholic music and art, the rain, and the way each night adds a new twist. Criticisms are about balance: upgrades after the first feel small, two ships arriving from opposite sides can be nearly impossible to save, and the last nights can feel opaque. Coverage is limited to jam comments.

    People like

    • Atmospheric music, rain sounds and art
    • Easy to understand, with new mechanics each night
    • Letters from the crews add feeling

    Common complaints

    • Later upgrades feel small
    • Some ship arrivals are very hard to handle at once
    • The last nights can feel opaque
