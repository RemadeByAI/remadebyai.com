    About HolyArrs

    HolyArrs does the jobs of Sonarr, Radarr, Lidarr, Bazarr, Prowlarr and Seerr in one app with one login. You browse it like a streaming service, people in your household ask for a title, and it searches your indexers, picks a release by your rules, sends it to qBittorrent, SABnzbd or another downloader, fetches subtitles and files it into your library. It has a calendar, alerts to Discord, Telegram, ntfy and others, and can import an existing Sonarr, Radarr, Lidarr, Prowlarr, Bazarr or Seerr setup without changing it.

    It runs as a small Docker container (about 16 MB) on amd64, arm64 or armv7, with an experimental Synology package too. Posters and search come from the maker's own metadata server, so no API keys are needed. The project went public in November 2026 and is still young.

    What people say

    HolyArrs got a busy Show HN launch and two setup guides within its first week. People like the idea of one small app with one login in place of six, and guide writers say it is worth trying next to an existing setup. The common worries are that it is very new and largely AI-written, that it depends on the maker's own metadata server, and that earlier attempts to merge the *arr apps faded away. Coverage so far is launch discussion and tutorials, not long-term use.

    People like

    • One app, one login and one port instead of six
    • Very small download, because it is a single Go program
    • Imports an existing Sonarr or Radarr setup without changing it, so it is easy to try side by side

    Common complaints

    • Very young, with many releases a day at launch, so behaviour can change
    • Relies on a metadata server run and paid for by the maker
    • Doubts about long-term maintenance of a largely AI-written project
    • Some feel the separate *arr apps exist for good reasons
