    Dorso

    Alternative to Upright and other posture apps

    A Mac menu bar app that watches your posture through the camera or AirPods and gradually blurs the screen when you slouch. Sit up straight and the blur clears.

    About Dorso

    Dorso sits in the menu bar and checks how you are sitting. With the camera it uses Apple's Vision framework to follow the position of your head against a baseline you calibrate while sitting up straight; with AirPods Pro, Max or 3rd generation and later it reads the tilt of your head instead, so no camera is needed. When you slouch, the screen blurs a little, then more the worse it gets. You can pick a glow, border or solid warning instead, set a dead zone, delay and sensitivity, pause it when you're away from your desk, and see a daily posture score with a seven-day chart.

    All processing happens on your Mac, with no account and no cloud. It needs macOS 13 Ventura or later (AirPods mode needs macOS 14). The signed and notarized download on GitHub and the Homebrew install are free; the same app is also sold on the Mac App Store as "Dorso - Posture Monitor" for $4.99. It was first released as Posturr.

    What people say

    Dorso got wide attention in January 2026, when it was still called Posturr: a Hacker News thread reached the front page and the German news agency dpa wrote it up. People who tried it said the gradual blur works and quickly changed how they sit. The criticism was about CPU use, unease with a camera that is always on, the app not opening on some Macs at first, and a debate over whether sitting upright is healthy at all. Most of this feedback is about the first release; later versions added AirPods tracking and a notarized download.

    People like

    • The gradual blur is an effective nudge to sit up
    • Users said it improved their posture within days
    • Runs locally with no account

    Common complaints

    • Early versions used a noticeable share of CPU
    • Some people are uneasy about the camera watching them all day
    • Mac only
    • Some commenters doubt that upright posture is the right goal
