    About Sill

    Sill for Mac sits in the menu bar and streams the window you pick, or the whole desktop, to the free Sill app on your iPhone or iPad as HEVC video over an encrypted connection. Taps, scrolls, three-finger gestures, keys and Apple Pencil go back to the Mac, the app's menus show up on the device, text can be shared through the clipboard both ways, and Mac sound can play along (a beta). A virtual display keeps the streamed window updating even when other windows cover it.

    Devices connect on the same network, over a USB cable, with a direct Wi-Fi link when there is no shared network, or from away through your own VPN such as Tailscale. You pair each device once. It needs macOS 14 or later (Apple silicon or Intel) and iOS or iPadOS 17 or later. The Mac app is a free notarized download (or brew install --cask saffsanity/sill/sill) and the iPhone and iPad app is free on the App Store.

    What people say

    Sill got a popular Hacker News thread in October 2026, and early users there and on the App Store say it feels faster and simpler than VNC-style remote desktop apps, and easier than DIY streaming setups. People like that it starts from the iPhone or iPad, which Apple's Sidecar does not do. The complaints are about early bugs: keyboard modifiers, trackpad scrolling and Apple Pencil glitches in some apps, plus a few people who found it hard to tell from the description that it is a remote desktop app. Coverage is mostly launch-time comments and short App Store reviews.

    People like

    • Faster and smoother than VNC remote desktop apps

    Common complaints

    • Early bugs with keyboard shortcuts and trackpad scrolling
