    About InCloseMail

    InCloseMail works like classic Outlook, screen by screen: the ribbon, folder pane, reading pane and To-Do Bar, Outlook's keyboard shortcuts and right-click menus, .pst import and export, Focused Inbox, server-side rules and Quick Steps, S/MIME signed and encrypted mail, and a calendar with the Scheduling Assistant, Room Finder and Teams meetings. People, Microsoft To Do tasks, shared mailboxes with Send As, aliases, Microsoft 365 Groups and profiles are all there, and settings and signatures follow you to other PCs.

    It adds things Outlook doesn't have. AI agents such as Claude (Claude Code connects in one click) can read, translate and draft mail in the mailboxes you choose, and anything they propose changes nothing until you click Apply. Clean Up My Inbox groups likely junk, newsletters, people you never answer and big old messages so you can act on thousands at once, with one Undo. You can remap any keyboard shortcut, run rules on folders of any size, blacklist a sender on every device in one click, see why a message looks suspicious, and get a second look before sending to outside recipients or forgetting an attachment.

    There is also Analytics about your own mail (kept on your PC), Lock InCloseMail with Windows Hello, read-only accounts, Dark Mode that darkens emailed charts, themes and a Theme Editor, sticky Notes shared across your PCs, RSS feeds and a Demo Mode with a sample mailbox. It keeps working offline during a Microsoft 365 outage, and comes in ten languages. IT gets a signed MSI with Group Policy and Intune templates.

    It is free forever for Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live and other personal Microsoft accounts. Microsoft 365 work accounts will cost $3 per user per month ($36 a year) after the beta, and are free during it; one licence covers one person on up to three PCs. The first public beta, version 26.11.2608, came out on November 26, 2026. It runs on 64-bit Windows 10 (version 1809 or later) and Windows 11.
