    About Compositor

    Compositor is made for compositing and photo touch-up the way Photoshop users already work: layers and folders with blend modes, layer masks and clipping masks, adjustment and filter layers, layer effects like drop shadow and glow, and non-destructive transforms. Selections include marquee, lasso, magic wand and Select Subject, and there are Spot Healing, Clone Stamp, brush, gradient, shape and type tools, a Camera Raw style panel, Content-Aware Fill and background removal. Shortcuts follow Photoshop's and can be remapped.

    It opens JPEG, PNG, HEIC, TIFF, WebP, SVG, PDF, GIF, camera RAW and 8-bit RGB Photoshop PSD and PSB files, keeping folders, masks and simple text editable, and exports PNG, JPEG, PDF and animated GIF. It needs macOS 26 or later on an Apple silicon Mac. Download it from the site or GitHub, or install it with Homebrew (brew install --cask robbietilton-compositor).

    What people say

    Mac news sites describe Compositor as a capable, very small (about 12 MB) free alternative to Photoshop for compositing and photo touch-ups, and readers who tried it liked how much easier it is than GIMP. Writers and readers agree it does not have everything Photoshop has. Reader comments point to its narrow system support (it needs a recent macOS on Apple silicon), and one German reader felt the software quality still has a way to go. Coverage is mostly short news write-ups with reader comments rather than in-depth reviews; the GitHub repo has about 18,800 stars.

    People like

    • Free, open source and very small compared with Photoshop
    • Powerful layer, mask and adjustment tools for compositing
    • More familiar and easier to use than GIMP for Photoshop users

    Common complaints

    • Does not have every Photoshop feature
    • Runs only on the newest macOS with Apple silicon, so older Macs are left out
    • A reader found no way to save a project, only export a JPEG
    • Some readers feel the software quality is still uneven
