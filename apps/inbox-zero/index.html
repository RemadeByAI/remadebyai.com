    About Inbox Zero

    The assistant works in the background on your Gmail or Outlook account. You tell it in plain English how to handle mail, and it labels and files messages, drafts replies in your tone, tracks what you still need to answer, blocks cold emails, bulk-archives and unsubscribes, briefs you before meetings and saves attachments to Google Drive or OneDrive. You can also chat with it from Slack or Telegram.

    The separate email client has a split inbox and all your accounts in one place, on the web and desktop, with iPhone and Android apps. You can self-host it with Docker or use the hosted version at getinboxzero.com.

    What people say

    There's less independent user discussion of Inbox Zero than its GitHub popularity suggests. The main real conversation is its December 2023 Show HN thread; most of the rest is Product Hunt launch comments and short review-site ratings. People like writing email rules in plain English, the pre-drafted replies, and how fast bulk unsubscribe clears an old inbox, and they value that it's open source and can be self-hosted. The biggest worry, on both Hacker News and Product Hunt, is privacy: handing a whole inbox to a cloud AI model makes many people uneasy (the maker's answer is to self-host). Later reviews add that the hosted plan is fairly pricey per user and self-hosting takes real effort.

    Ratings are high (4.8 on Product Hunt, 5.0 on G2) but come from only a handful of reviews.

    People like

    • Plain-English AI rules and pre-drafted replies stand out
    • Bulk unsubscribe and archive clear years-old inboxes in a few clicks
    • Open source and free to self-host, which reassures privacy-minded users
    • The developer ships fixes and features quickly

    Common complaints

    • Privacy: many are uneasy sending a whole inbox to a third-party AI model
    • The hosted plan is fairly expensive per user, with only a short trial
    • Self-hosting takes real effort (hosting, API keys, upgrades)
    • Early versions were Gmail-only (Outlook came later)
