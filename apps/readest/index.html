    About Readest

    Readest is a rewrite of the Foliate reader that runs everywhere. It reads EPUB, PDF, MOBI, KF8 (AZW3), FB2, CBZ, TXT, HTML and Markdown, in scrolling or page view, with highlights, bookmarks and notes, dictionary and Wikipedia lookup, full-text search, split-screen parallel reading and read-along narration. It connects to OPDS and Calibre libraries and can sync progress with KOReader devices.

    It is in the App Store, Google Play and Flathub, with downloads for Windows and Linux and a web version. The app is free; optional Plus ($4.99 a month) and Pro ($9.99 a month) plans add more cloud sync storage, more AI translations and DeepL Pro.

    What people say

    Most readers who write about Readest like it a lot. They call it one of the best cross-platform ebook readers, praising the clean look, detailed font and layout controls, a big free feature set with no ads, and progress sync that also works with KOReader and Calibre. Reviewers note rough edges: occasional slow loading or crashes, text-to-speech glitches (especially with PDFs), and desktop quirks such as a click turning the page. A newer group of critics dislikes the AI features and feels development favours quantity over polish; the developer disputes that.

    It scores well where it's rated (4.5 on the App Store, 4.6 on AlternativeTo), but Hacker News has said little about it.

    People like

    • Clean, modern design with very detailed font and layout controls
    • Generous free features (highlights, notes, search, parallel reading), no ads
    • Truly cross-platform, with sync that works alongside KOReader and Calibre
    • Actively developed, and the developer responds to reports

    Common complaints

    • Occasional slow loading, with some crash reports
    • Text-to-speech glitches, like skipped lines in PDFs
    • Desktop quirks, such as clicks turning the page or a forgotten window size
    • Free AI and translation are capped, and some see the AI features as bloat
