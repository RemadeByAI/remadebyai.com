    About Bitchat

    Nearby phones relay messages to each other over Bluetooth Low Energy, up to seven hops, so a group can keep chatting at a protest, a festival or after a disaster with no signal. When you are online it reaches further through Nostr relays, including location channels for your block, neighbourhood or city. Private messages are end-to-end encrypted with the Noise protocol, and a triple tap wipes everything.

    The iPhone and Mac app is in the App Store, and the Android app is on Google Play and GitHub. It launched in July 2025 and has had wide press coverage; Dorsey has said it had no external security review at launch.

    What people say

    People like the idea behind Bitchat: a messenger with no account or phone number that works offline over a Bluetooth mesh. Commenters keep pointing to censorship, internet shutdowns and disasters as where it matters, and App Store users rate it highly for having no sign-up and no ads. The launch in July 2025 drew sharp criticism from security researchers, who showed that contacts could be impersonated. Dorsey added a warning not to rely on its security, and Trail of Bits, while crediting the quick fixes and the move to the Noise protocol, advised against using it for sensitive conversations at that time.

    In everyday use, the common complaints are short Bluetooth range and too few people nearby to form a mesh. Some also worry about battery drain.
