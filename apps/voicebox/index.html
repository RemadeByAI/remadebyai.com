    Alternative to ElevenLabs and Wispr Flow

    About Voicebox

    Voicebox covers both halves of voice: speech out, like ElevenLabs, and dictation in, like Wispr Flow. It has eight text-to-speech engines, zero-shot voice cloning and 50+ preset voices, effects such as pitch, reverb and compression, and no length limit. A global hotkey transcribes your speech with Whisper and pastes it into whatever you are typing in. Its MCP server lets AI agents such as Claude Code or Cursor speak to you in a voice you choose.

    Models and voice data never leave your machine. There are installers for Apple silicon and Intel Macs and for Windows; Linux users build from source.

    What people say

    Most of what's written about Voicebox is enthusiastic: people see it as a free, local-first alternative to ElevenLabs, and like that voice samples never leave their own computer and that several speech engines sit behind one tidy app. Hands-on criticism comes mostly from GitHub issues: early Windows builds didn't use the GPU, first-run guidance was thin, and the open-issue list is long. It also needs a fairly strong machine. Independent tests of its quality against ElevenLabs are still scarce.

    Hacker News coverage is light (mostly passing recommendations), and we couldn't check Reddit for this round-up.

    People like

    • Runs fully on your own computer: no per-character billing, no rate limits, private voice data
    • Several speech engines, voice cloning and dictation in one app
    • Free and MIT-licensed, with extras like a multi-speaker story editor and an MCP server for AI agents
    • Polished interface and well-thought-out dictation

    Common complaints

    • Early Windows installers didn't use the GPU unless you built from source
    • Bugs and thin onboarding reported, with a large issue backlog
    • Hardware-hungry: multi-GB model downloads, about 8 GB VRAM or 16 GB unified memory recommended
    • Features vary by engine, and speed/quality claims aren't independently tested
